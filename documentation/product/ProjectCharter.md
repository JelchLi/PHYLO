# Project Charter

**Project:** PHYLO (Physics Learning Openware)

## Purpose

PHYLO is an open educational platform that enables teachers, students, educational institutions, and contributors to access, build, use, and improve practical science laboratories through openly available hardware, software, documentation, and learning resources.

The project promotes hands-on experimentation by making laboratory experiences affordable, reproducible, and accessible using locally available materials and open technologies.

Although the initial implementation focuses on secondary-school physics, the platform is intentionally designed to support future expansion into additional scientific disciplines, educational levels, and collaborative initiatives.

---

# Mission

Develop an open educational platform that enables teachers and students to access, build, use, and improve affordable open-source physics laboratories through freely available hardware designs, software, documentation, and educational resources.

---

# Vision

Enable any educational institution in Latin America to reproduce high-quality experimental laboratories using locally available materials through an open, collaborative, and continuously evolving educational platform.

---

# Project Identity

**PHYLO** stands for **Physics Learning Openware**.

The name reflects three fundamental characteristics of the project:

* Physics as the initial educational focus.
* Learning as the primary objective.
* Openware as the commitment to openly shared knowledge, including software, hardware, documentation, and educational resources.

The name also resonates with the concept of *philo* (love of knowledge), representing curiosity, continuous learning, and scientific exploration.

---

# Target Users

## Teachers

Teachers who seek affordable, reproducible laboratory experiences supported by teaching guides, downloadable resources, assembly instructions, and digital tools.

## Students

Students who perform experiments, collect and analyze data, and strengthen conceptual understanding through real laboratory activities.

## Educational Institutions

Schools, universities, research groups, educational foundations, and organizations interested in adopting, adapting, or contributing to PHYLO.

## Contributors

Developers, designers, educators, researchers, and makers interested in improving the platform, creating new laboratories, expanding documentation, or contributing to the open-source ecosystem.

---

# User Experience Goals

PHYLO should create an experience where educators immediately perceive that practical science education can be accessible.

The platform should encourage exploration while remaining intuitive and professional.

An ideal user journey is:

1. Discover a collection of freely available educational resources.
2. Feel inspired by the quality and openness of the platform.
3. Realize that the laboratories are feasible to build.
4. Download documentation and resources with confidence.
5. Successfully reproduce laboratory experiences in the classroom.
6. Return to the platform for additional laboratories and future updates.

---

# Core Principles

## Open Source

Whenever possible, software, hardware designs, documentation, and educational materials should be openly available.

## Reproducibility

Laboratories should be designed using affordable and locally available materials so they can be reproduced by educational institutions with limited resources.

## Hands-on Learning

PHYLO prioritizes experimentation with real physical systems over purely virtual experiences.

## Scalability

The platform should be designed to support continuous growth, allowing new laboratories, applications, educational resources, and scientific disciplines to be incorporated without major architectural changes.

---

# Initial Scope

The first public version of PHYLO will provide:

* Laboratory documentation
* Teacher manuals
* Student manuals
* Open hardware files
* Firmware
* Laboratory web applications
* Assembly guides
* Downloadable resources

Future versions may incorporate:

* Community contributions
* User accounts
* Interactive simulations
* Learning analytics
* Additional scientific disciplines
* Collaborative educational content

---

# Success Criteria

PHYLO will be considered successful when educational institutions can independently reproduce laboratory experiences using openly available resources and integrate them into classroom teaching with minimal technical barriers.

Success is measured not only by software quality, but also by educational impact, accessibility, reproducibility, and community participation.

---

# Project Evolution

PHYLO is intended to evolve as a long-term educational ecosystem.

The architecture, documentation, and software should support continuous expansion while preserving the project's founding principles of openness, accessibility, reproducibility, and practical learning.
